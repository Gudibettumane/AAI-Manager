### Q-PWR-001 `[AAI-JE-EE-2018]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Transmission Line Phenomena — Ferranti Effect
**Question:** The Ferranti effect in a transmission line is experienced when:
- (A) The line is heavily loaded with lagging pf load
- (B) The line is operating at unity pf full load
- (C) The line is long and open-circuited or lightly loaded
- (D) A symmetrical 3-phase fault occurs
**Answer:** (C)
**Concept/Formula:** In long or medium transmission lines under no-load or light load, charging current through shunt capacitance produces a voltage drop across series inductance that leads to $V_R > V_S$.
**Remedy:** Install shunt reactors at the receiving end.

### Q-PWR-002 `[ESE-EE-2020]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Surge Impedance Loading (SIL)
**Question:** A $400\text{ kV}$, 3-phase lossless overhead transmission line has a surge impedance $Z_c = 400\ \Omega$. The Surge Impedance Loading (SIL) of the line is:
- (A) $100\text{ MW}$
- (B) $400\text{ MW}$
- (C) $800\text{ MW}$
- (D) $1600\text{ MW}$
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- $\text{SIL} = \frac{V_L^2}{Z_c} = \frac{(400\text{ kV})^2}{400\ \Omega} = \frac{160000}{400} = 400\text{ MW}$.
- Note: At SIL loading, reactive power generated by line capacitance exactly equals reactive power consumed by line inductance ($Q_{net} = 0$).

### Q-PWR-003 `[GATE-EE-2018]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Symmetrical Components & Sequence Networks
**Question:** A single line-to-ground (L-G) fault occurs on phase A of an unloaded generator with neutral solidly grounded. The relationship between the sequence currents of phase A is:
- (A) $I_{a1} = I_{a2} = I_{a0} = \frac{I_f}{3}$
- (B) $I_{a1} + I_{a2} + I_{a0} = 0$
- (C) $I_{a1} = -I_{a2}$ and $I_{a0} = 0$
- (D) $I_{a1} = I_{a2}$ and $I_{a0} = -2 I_{a1}$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- In an L-G fault, the positive, negative, and zero sequence networks are connected in **SERIES**.
- Therefore, $I_{a1} = I_{a2} = I_{a0} = \frac{E_a}{Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0 + 3Z_n} = \frac{I_f}{3}$.

### Q-PWR-004 `[ESE-EE-2019]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Unsymmetrical Fault Severity
**Question:** Which of the following faults is the most frequent in power transmission systems, and which is the most severe?
- (A) Most frequent: 3-Phase fault; Most severe: Single line-to-ground fault
- (B) Most frequent: Single line-to-ground fault; Most severe: 3-Phase fault
- (C) Most frequent: Line-to-line fault; Most severe: Double line-to-ground fault
- (D) Most frequent: Double line-to-ground fault; Most severe: Line-to-line fault
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Frequency: L-G (~70-80%) > L-L (~15%) > L-L-G (~10%) > 3-Phase (~2-5%).
- Severity / Fault Current: Symmetrical 3-Phase fault generally produces the highest fault MVA (except occasionally L-G when generator neutral is solidly grounded with $Z_0 < Z_1$).

### Q-PWR-005 `[AAI-JE-EE-2018]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Transformer Protection — Buchholz Relay
**Question:** The Buchholz relay is mounted:
- (A) In the main tank of the transformer
- (B) In the conservator tank
- (C) On the connecting pipe between the main tank and the conservator tank
- (D) Across the high-voltage bushings
**Answer:** (C)
**Concept/Formula:** Gas-actuated relay located on the pipe between the transformer tank and conservator tank; detects incipient faults (slow gas accumulation gives alarm) and severe internal faults (oil surge trips circuit breaker).

### Q-PWR-006 `[GATE-EE-2017]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Circuit Breakers & Restriking Voltage
**Question:** In a circuit breaker, the Rate of Rise of Restriking Voltage (RRRV) is maximum when:
- (A) $\sin(\omega_n t) = 1$, i.e., $t = \frac{\pi}{2 \omega_n}$
- (B) $\cos(\omega_n t) = 1$, i.e., $t = 0$
- (C) $t = \frac{\pi}{\omega_n}$
- (D) $t = \infty$
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Restriking voltage $v_r(t) = V_m (1 - \cos \omega_n t)$, where $\omega_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.
- $\text{RRRV} = \frac{d v_r}{dt} = V_m \omega_n \sin(\omega_n t)$.
- Maximum RRRV occurs when $\sin(\omega_n t) = 1 \implies \text{RRRV}_{max} = V_m \omega_n = \frac{V_m}{\sqrt{LC}}$.
- Peak restriking voltage is $2 V_m$, occurring at $t = \frac{\pi}{\omega_n}$.
