### Q-CON-001 `[ESE-GS-2020 / CPWD-Manual]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Contract Management — Tendering & Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)
**Question:** In government engineering tenders (including AAI and CPWD works), Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) is collected from bidders primarily to:
- (A) Guarantee that the contractor finishes the job within budget
- (B) Ensure bona fide bidding and deter frivolous bidders who might withdraw or modify their tender after bid opening
- (C) Cover the cost of printing tender documents
- (D) Pay the contractor an advance mobilization fund
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- EMD (typically 2% of the estimated cost of work put to tender) guarantees that the bidder will not back out upon winning. It is refunded to unsuccessful bidders.
- **Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG)** (typically 3% to 5% of contract value) is submitted by the winning contractor prior to signing the contract to ensure faithful execution of the works.
- **Security Deposit (SD)** (typically 2.5% to 5%) is deducted from running bills and retained until the end of the Defect Liability Period (DLP).

### Q-CON-002 `[ESE-GS-2019]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Project Planning — Critical Path Method (CPM)
**Question:** In CPM network analysis, the "Critical Path" is defined as:
- (A) The path with the maximum number of activities
- (B) The longest duration path through the network, where total float for every activity is zero
- (C) The shortest path through the network
- (D) The path with the maximum cost
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- The critical path dictates the minimum possible time required to complete the entire project.
- Activities on the critical path have zero total float ($\text{TF} = L_j - E_i - t_{ij} = 0$). Any delay in a critical activity directly delays the project completion date.

### Q-CON-003 `[ESE-GS-2021]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Project Planning — PERT Three-Time Estimates
**Question:** In Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT), if an activity has optimistic time $t_o = 4\text{ days}$, most likely time $t_m = 7\text{ days}$, and pessimistic time $t_p = 16\text{ days}$, the expected duration ($t_e$) and variance ($\sigma^2$) are:
- (A) $t_e = 8\text{ days},\ \sigma^2 = 4\text{ days}^2$
- (B) $t_e = 7\text{ days},\ \sigma^2 = 2\text{ days}^2$
- (C) $t_e = 9\text{ days},\ \sigma^2 = 4\text{ days}^2$
- (D) $t_e = 8\text{ days},\ \sigma^2 = 2\text{ days}^2$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Expected time (Beta distribution mean):
  $$t_e = \frac{t_o + 4 t_m + t_p}{6} = \frac{4 + 4(7) + 16}{6} = \frac{4 + 28 + 16}{6} = \frac{48}{6} = 8\text{ days}$$
- Standard deviation: $\sigma = \frac{t_p - t_o}{6} = \frac{16 - 4}{6} = \frac{12}{6} = 2\text{ days}$.
- Variance: $\sigma^2 = 2^2 = 4\text{ days}^2$.

### Q-CON-004 `[CEA-Reg-2010 / IE-Rules]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Electrical Safety Codes — Maximum Permissible Voltage Variation
**Question:** According to Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations / IE Rule 54, the declared supply voltage to consumers should not vary from rated voltage by more than:
- (A) $\pm 6\%$ in case of Low or Medium Voltage
- (B) $\pm 15\%$ in case of Low Voltage
- (C) $\pm 1\%$ in case of High Voltage
- (D) $\pm 12.5\%$ in all cases
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Low and Medium Voltage (up to $650\text{ V}$): $\pm 6\%$.
- High Voltage ($650\text{ V} \text{ to } 33\text{ kV}$): $+6\%$ to $-9\%$.
- Extra High Voltage ($> 33\text{ kV}$): $+10\%$ to $-12.5\%$ (or $\pm 10\%$).
- Frequency: $\pm 3\%$ (i.e., $48.5\text{ Hz to } 51.5\text{ Hz}$).

### Q-CON-005 `[NBC-2016 / IS-732]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** National Building Code (NBC 2016) — Substation & Electrical Rooms
**Question:** According to NBC 2016 Part 8 (Building Services), transformers with oil filling exceeding $2000\text{ litres}$ located inside a building or basement must be:
- (A) Provided with an automatic nitrogen injection fire prevention system or installed outside / in a separate dedicated substation building with a 4-hour fire separation wall
- (B) Operated without any fire barriers
- (C) Painted yellow
- (D) Cooled by water mist only
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:** For large passenger terminals (airports), NBC 2016 strongly mandates **Dry-Type (Cast Resin) Transformers** for indoor substations. If oil-filled transformers are used, strict soak pits, pebble drains, fire-rated blast doors, and fire suppression walls (minimum 4-hour fire rating) are legally compulsory.

### Q-CON-006 `[ESE-GS-2022 / CPWD-Manual]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Contract Management — Types of Engineering Contracts
**Question:** In airport EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) turnkey contracts, which party primarily bears the financial risk of design discrepancies, quantities variance, and site-condition coordination?
- (A) The Employer / Client (AAI) entirely
- (B) The Turnkey EPC Contractor
- (C) The Sub-contractors equally
- (D) The Statutory Inspection Authority
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- In an **EPC / Turnkey Contract**, single-point responsibility rests with the contractor for detailed engineering, procurement, construction, testing, and commissioning at a fixed lumpsum price. The contractor absorbs design and quantity escalation risks.
- In contrast, in an **Item-Rate Contract (Unit Price Contract)**, the employer prepares the BOQ (Bill of Quantities); the contractor quotes unit rates, and final payment is made on actual physical measurements verified by the Engineer-in-Charge.

### Q-CON-007 `[CPWD-Works-Manual / ESE-GS]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Contract Administration — Liquidated Damages vs Penalty
**Question:** In public works contracts (CPWD/AAI), compensation for delay (Liquidated Damages) levied on a defaulting contractor is:
- (A) A punitive criminal fine imposed without contractual justification
- (B) A genuine pre-estimate of agreed financial loss suffered by the employer due to delayed completion, typically capped at 10% of the tendered contract value
- (C) 50% of the contract value in all cases
- (D) Collected only if the contractor files for bankruptcy
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Liquidated Damages (LD) represent legally enforceable pre-estimated liquidated compensation (typically computed as $0.5\%$ to $1.0\%$ per week of delay, subject to an overall ceiling of $10\%$ of tendered contract value).
- It is deducted by the Engineer-in-Charge from running account bills or security deposits.

### Q-CON-008 `[ESE-GS-2018]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Project Planning — Float Analysis in CPM Networks
**Question:** For an activity $(i-j)$ with duration $t_{ij} = 5\text{ days}$, early start time of event $i$ is $E_i = 10$, late event time of event $i$ is $L_i = 12$, early event time of event $j$ is $E_j = 20$, and late event time of event $j$ is $L_j = 22$. The Total Float ($TF$) and Free Float ($FF$) are:
- (A) $TF = 7\text{ days},\ FF = 5\text{ days}$
- (B) $TF = 5\text{ days},\ FF = 7\text{ days}$
- (C) $TF = 7\text{ days},\ FF = 7\text{ days}$
- (D) $TF = 2\text{ days},\ FF = 0\text{ days}$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Total Float: $TF = L_j - E_i - t_{ij} = 22 - 10 - 5 = 7\text{ days}$.
- Free Float: $FF = E_j - E_i - t_{ij} = 20 - 10 - 5 = 5\text{ days}$.
- Independent Float: $IF = E_j - L_i - t_{ij} = 20 - 12 - 5 = 3\text{ days}$ (subject to $\ge 0$).
- Key property: $TF \ge FF \ge IF \ge 0$.

### Q-CON-009 `[ESE-GS-2017]` 🔴 Tricky
**Topic:** Project Management — Crashing of Project Networks & Cost Slope
**Question:** When crashing an activity to compress project duration, the "Cost Slope" representing the incremental direct cost incurred per unit time crashed is given by:
- (A) $\frac{\text{Crash Cost} - \text{Normal Cost}}{\text{Normal Time} - \text{Crash Time}}$
- (B) $\frac{\text{Normal Cost} - \text{Crash Cost}}{\text{Normal Time} - \text{Crash Time}}$
- (C) $\frac{\text{Normal Time} - \text{Crash Time}}{\text{Crash Cost} - \text{Normal Cost}}$
- (D) $(\text{Crash Cost} \times \text{Normal Cost}) / (\text{Normal Time})$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
$$\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta t} = \frac{C_c - C_n}{t_n - t_c}$$
- To crash a project network optimally at minimum extra expense, crash the activity lying on the **critical path** having the **lowest cost slope**.
- Crashing increases direct project costs but reduces indirect overhead costs.

- (B) 20 points or 2000 Watts

### Q-CON-011 `[CEA-Reg-2010 / IE-Rules]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Electrical Safety Regulations — Insulation Resistance Requirement
**Question:** Under Regulation 34 of CEA Regulations 2010 (and IE Rule 48), the insulation resistance between phase conductors and earth for an electric installation operating at medium voltage ($415\text{ V}$) before energization shall NOT be less than:
- (A) $1\text{ M}\Omega$
- (B) $0.1\text{ M}\Omega$
- (C) $50\text{ M}\Omega$
- (D) $100\text{ \Omega}$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- CEA Reg 34 / IE Rule 48 stipulates: The insulation resistance of the installation measured with a $500\text{ V}$ or $1000\text{ V}$ DC Megger shall not be less than **$1\text{ M}\Omega$** (or calculated as $\frac{50}{\text{Number of points}}\text{ M}\Omega$, minimum $1\text{ M}\Omega$).
- Maximum permissible leakage current to earth shall not exceed **$1/5000\text{th}$** ($0.02\%$) of the maximum supply load current.

### Q-CON-012 `[IS-3043 / CEA-2010]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Earthing Codes & Practice — Permissible Earth Resistance Limits
**Question:** According to IS:3043 and CEA Regulations, what is the recommended maximum permissible value of combined earth electrode resistance for a large generating station or $33\text{ kV} / 11\text{ kV}$ electrical substation?
- (A) Not exceeding $0.5\text{ }\Omega$ to $1.0\text{ }\Omega$
- (B) Not exceeding $10\text{ }\Omega$
- (C) Between $25\text{ }\Omega$ and $50\text{ }\Omega$
- (D) Exactly zero ohms
**Answer:** (A)

### Q-CON-013 `[NBC-2016-Part8 / CPWD]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Building Services — Emergency Egress Lighting & Signage
**Question:** Under NBC 2016 Part 8, what is the statutory duration for which emergency exit signs and escape route illumination must operate automatically upon total failure of the normal mains supply?
- (A) Minimum 90 minutes (1.5 hours)
- (B) Minimum 10 minutes
- (C) 12 hours
- (D) 5 minutes
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Emergency lighting along exit routes, stairways, and passenger terminal corridors must automatically ignite within **$1\text{ to } 5\text{ seconds}$** of mains failure and sustain a minimum illuminance of **$1\text{ lux}$** along the centerline of egress paths for at least **$90\text{ minutes}$** (or $3\text{ hours}$ in critical transit hubs) powered by dedicated battery packs or central inverter systems.

### Q-CON-014 `[CPWD-Works-Manual]` 🟢 Easy
**Topic:** Contract Management — Defect Liability Period (DLP)
**Question:** In CPWD and AAI engineering works contracts, the Defect Liability Period (DLP) typically starts from:
- (A) The date of award of the tender
- (B) The date of recording the Certificate of Virtual/Final Completion of work
- (C) The date when the contractor submits the first running bill
- (D) The date of bid opening
**Answer:** (B)
**Concept/Formula:**
- DLP (normally 12 months for civil/electrical works, and up to 24–36 months for specialized MEP like chillers, lifts, DG sets) commences strictly from the certified date of completion.
- During this window, any manufacturing defects, poor workmanship, or failures must be rectified by the contractor at their own cost, failing which the security deposit is forfeited.

### Q-CON-015 `[CEA-Reg-2010]` 🟡 Moderate
**Topic:** Electrical Safety — Clearances Around High Voltage Switchgear
**Question:** According to CEA Regulations (Regulation 35 / 37), what is the minimum clear passage width required in front of an indoor High Voltage switchboard panel where operating handles and instruments are situated?
- (A) Not less than $1.2\text{ m}$ (or $1.5\text{ m}$ if panels face each other)
- (B) Not less than $0.3\text{ m}$
- (C) Not less than $3.0\text{ m}$
- (D) $0.6\text{ m}$
**Answer:** (A)
**Concept/Formula:**
- Clear operating space in front of an LT switchboard: Minimum $0.9\text{ m}$ to $1.0\text{ m}$.
- Clear space in front of HT Switchboards: Minimum **$1.2\text{ m}$** (single row) or **$1.5\text{ m}$** (when switchboards face each other).
- Minimum clear passage behind the switchboard (for rear maintenance): Minimum **$0.75\text{ m}$** (or $0.9\text{ m}$).
